````text
Deep generative models have advanced a lot recently and this has led to striking applications, for example image inpainting, style transfer, image superresolution and text-to-image translation. But compared to the supervised setting there is a noticeable gap when it comes to fairness in generative models and in conditional generative models.
````

````text
In this work we focus on conditional generative models and we investigate their fairness and diversity. We introduce a new set of metrics that can be used as a practical tool to assess fairness violations of conditional generative models. The metrics are connected to the fairness definitions introduced in earlier work, in the sense that fairness is achieved if and only if our fairness violation metrics are equal to zero. Using these metrics we build a comprehensive framework, which supplements the standard performance criteria with fairness and diversity metrics. Based on the metrics we also design a versatile benchmark, so that the performance, fairness and diversity of a wide variety of conditional generative models can be evaluated in a unified way.

To show what the framework can do we evaluate one particular type of conditional generative modeling, image upsampling, which has triggered a discussion about racial biases in deep generative models. We use the FairFace dataset to introduce UnfairFace, which is a subset of FairFace that mimics the distribution of common large-scale face datasets. FairFace has race labels, so we can do an in-depth empirical evaluation of a range of upsampling methods and how dataset bias influences them. The evaluation shows that none of the methods are significantly fair, but how fair they are varies significantly between the methods. As expected, a racial bias in the training data makes most models less fair. The effect is most apparent for Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models (DDRM), which have the highest discrepancy to fairness of all the models we looked at when they are trained on UnfairFace, but the lowest discrepancy to fairness when they are trained on FairFace. On the other hand the fairness discrepancy of Posterior Sampling stays robust even when the training data is biased. These findings show that careful data acquisition is important and that biases in the dataset can impact which model is selected.

## Notation

We define performance and fairness using probabilistic terminology. Target samples are denoted by x\in\mathcal{X} and conditional arguments by y\in\mathcal{Y}. The target samples and the conditions follow the distribution \mathbb{P}_{x,y}, which has the marginals \mathbb{P}_{x} and \mathbb{P}_{y}. The conditional distribution of y given a target x is \mathbb{P}_{y|x}, and the conditional distribution of x given a condition y is \mathbb{P}_{x|y}. The conditional generative model generates samples \hat{x} conditioned on y, and these follow the distribution \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y}. If we marginalize \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y} over all y we get the distribution \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}}. We also denote by \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|x} the conditional distribution we get by marginalizing the product \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{y|x} over all y. To make things clearer we use the notation \mathbb{P}{}(\hat{x}\in A|\,x\in B):=\mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|x}(A|\,B) for measurable sets A,\,B in the domain of \hat{x} and x. We use similar notation for the other distributions here.
````

<details><summary><b>Step 7 — revised_draft</b> (1529 words)</summary>

````text
Remarkable advances in deep generative models (e.g. [1,2,3,4]) have given rise to striking applications, including image inpainting (e.g. [5]), style transfer (e.g. [6]), image superresolution (e.g. [7]), and text-to-image translation (e.g. [8]). Despite their apparent success, our understanding of these models and of the biases they are subject to lags behind, often revealing unexpected fairness violations. Earlier research on fairness in deep learning discovered biases in supervised prediction models, leading to racist [9,10], sexist [11,12], and homophobic [13] decisions, which can be critical in many applications such as criminal justice, medicine, hiring, or personalized advertising. These observations sparked a general interest in a research field concentrating on developing and quantifying fair machine learning models [14,15,16,10]. However, compared to the supervised setting, there is a noticeable gap in addressing fairness in generative models [17,18] and conditional generative models [19,20].

In this work, we focus on the latter and investigate conditional generative models with regard to their fairness and diversity. We introduce a novel set of metrics that serves as a practical tool for assessing fairness violations of conditional generative models. These metrics are associated with the fairness definitions introduced by [19], in the sense that fairness is achieved if and only if our fairness violation metrics equal zero. The metrics form the foundation of a comprehensive framework that supplements standard performance criteria with fairness and diversity metrics. Based on them, we design a versatile benchmark that facilitates a unified evaluation of the performance, fairness, and diversity of a wide variety of conditional generative models. We showcase the capacities of the proposed framework by evaluating one particular type of conditional generative modeling, namely image upsampling, which has triggered a compelling discussion on racial biases in deep generative models [21]. We leverage the FairFace dataset [22] to introduce UnfairFace, a subset of FairFace that mimics the distribution of common large-scale face datasets. Since FairFace provides race labels, it allows us to conduct an in-depth empirical evaluation of a range of upsampling methods and their influence on dataset bias. Our evaluation demonstrates that while none of the methods is significantly fair, the degree of fairness varies significantly across methods. Unsurprisingly, a racial bias in the training data reduces the fairness of most models. The effect is most apparent for Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models (DDRM [25]), which exhibit the highest discrepancy to fairness among all investigated models when trained on UnfairFace, but the least discrepancy to fairness when trained on FairFace. In contrast, the fairness discrepancy of Posterior Sampling [19] remains robust despite biased training data. These findings highlight the importance of careful data acquisition and show how biases present in the dataset can impact model selection.

## Notation

In this work, we define performance and fairness using probabilistic terminology. We denote target samples by x\in\mathcal{X} and conditional arguments by y\in\mathcal{Y}. Target samples and conditions follow the distribution \mathbb{P}_{x,y} with marginals \mathbb{P}_{x} and \mathbb{P}_{y}, respectively. \mathbb{P}_{y|x} denotes the conditional distribution of y given a target x, and \mathbb{P}_{x|y} the conditional distribution of x given a condition y. The conditional generative model generates samples \hat{x} conditioned on y, which follow the distribution \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y}. We denote the distribution of \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y} marginalized over all y by \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}}. Moreover, we denote by \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|x} the conditional distribution obtained by marginalizing the product \mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|y}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{y|x} over all y. For clarity, we use the notation \mathbb{P}{}(\hat{x}\in A|\,x\in B):=\mathbb{P}_{\hat{x}|x}(A|\,B) for measurable sets A,\,B in the domain of \hat{x} and x, respectively, and a similar notation for all other distributions introduced here.
````

````text
Remarkable advancements in deep generative models (e.g. [1,2,3,4]) give rise to striking applications, including image inpainting (e.g. [5]), style transfer (e.g. [6]), image superresolution (e.g. [7]), and text-to-image translation (e.g. [8]). Despite their apparent success, our understanding of these models and the biases they are subject to lags behind, often revealing unexpected fairness violations. Earlier research on fairness in deep learning discovered biases in supervised prediction models, leading to racist [9,10], sexist [11,12], and homophobic [13] decisions, which can be critical in many applications, such as criminal justice, medicine, hiring, or personalized advertising. These observations sparked a general interest in a research field that concentrates on developing and quantifying fair machine learning models [14,15,16,10]. However, compared to the supervised setting, there is a noticeable gap in addressing fairness in generative models [17,18] and conditional generative models [19,20].

In this work, we focus on the latter and investigate conditional generative models regarding their fairness and diversity. We introduce a novel set of metrics that serves as a practical tool for assessing fairness violations of conditional generative models. These metrics are associated with the fairness definitions introduced by [19] in the sense that fairness is achieved if and only if our fairness violation metrics equal zero. These metrics form the foundation of a comprehensive framework that supplements standard performance criteria with fairness and diversity metrics. Based on these metrics, we design a versatile benchmark, facilitating a unified evaluation of the performance, fairness, and diversity of a wide variety of conditional generative models. We showcase the capacities of the proposed framework by evaluating one particular type of conditional generative modeling, namely image upsampling, which has triggered a compelling discussion on racial biases in deep generative models [21]. We leverage the FairFace dataset [22] to introduce UnfairFace, a subset of FairFace mimicking the distribution of common large-scale face datasets. Because FairFace provides race labels, it allows us to conduct an in-depth empirical evaluation of a range of upsampling methods and their influence on dataset bias. Our evaluation demonstrates that, while none of the methods are significantly fair, the degree of fairness varies significantly across methods. Unsurprisingly, we find that a racial bias in the training data reduces the fairness of most models. Notably, the effect is most apparent for Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models (DDRM [25]), which exhibit the highest discrepancy to fairness among all investigated models when trained on UnfairFace, but the least discrepancy to fairness when trained on FairFace. In contrast, the fairness discrepancy of Posterior Sampling [19] remains robust despite biased training data. These findings highlight the importance of careful data acquisition and emphasize how biases present in the dataset can impact model selection.

## Notation

In this work, we define performance and fairness using probabilistic terminology. Specifically, we denote target samples by x\in\mathcal{X} and conditional arguments by y\in\mathcal{Y}. Target samples and conditions follow the distribution \mathbb{P}_{x,y} with marginals \mathbb{P}_{x} and \mathbb{P}_{y}, respectively. \mathbb{P}_{y|x} denotes the conditional distribution of y given a target x and \mathbb{P}_{x|y} the conditional distribution of x given a condition y. We use a similar notation for all other distributions introduced in this paragraph.
````
