````text
Autonomous mobile manipulation is an important research area in robotics. It has many applications, e.g., manufacturing, warehousing, construction and household assistance. A popular but challenging task setting in mobile manipulation research requires the agent to actively observe and explore an unseen environment, and the goal is to manipulate a target object. Because the environment is unseen, the agent cannot just plan a trajectory directly to reach the object and grasp it. Instead it has to rely on online observations and scene priors to succeed, and this raises many research questions for the area.

Many existing works solve this problem by combining 3D reconstruction and scene geometry analysis with motion planning. But these approaches usually have huge computational costs for modeling the scene geometry, and they need complicated heuristic designs that are tailored to specific robots. Recently, reinforcement learning (RL) based approaches got more attention because they are simple and efficient. Some works use visibility and reachability as scene priors, which drive the agent to observe the target object and approach it. They learn these priors and use them in the policy's input states and also in the reward, and this improved the policy performance significantly.

In this work we also use an RL-based approach, and we propose a new scene prior, graspability, to improve mobile manipulation policy learning. Graspability is defined as the complete set of valid grasping poses of the target object. Reachability and visibility only give circuitous guidance to the agent for grasping, while graspability gives more direct and informative guidance for effective grasping.

Graspability contains the full information of the valid graspings of the target object, but estimating it online in an unseen environment is far from trivial. For example, online observations during mobile manipulation often have many occlusions and large overlaps, and this leads to noise and redundancy in the grasping pose predictions. So we propose an online grasping pose fusion module. It dynamically fuses the redundant grasping poses and it removes the outlier poses. The fusion gives a high-quality graspability estimation with high precision and recall of valid grasping poses.

We use the estimated graspability in two ways to help agent learning. First, we encode graspability into states and use it as policy input, so the agent is aware of the grasping goals. We find that the graspability-aware agent learns to move its base and arm more intelligently. Second, we use the number of grasps and the distance-to-grasp information in graspability as RL reward, which encourages the agent to get more observations of valid grasping poses. We also introduce a weight schedule to combine these two rewards, to balance the observation goal and the grasping goal. With this reward the agent prioritizes extensive observations in the initial stages, and then it shifts its focus to grasping the target object.

We do extensive experiments on two mainstream simulators, Habitat and Isaac Gym, with a diverse range of environments and objects, and show that our method outperforms mainstream methods on both an abstract grasping metric and a realistic grasping pose metric. We also evaluate in the real world, and this further shows the robustness and effectiveness of our method.

In summary, our contributions are:

- An online grasping fusion module that fuses predicted grasping poses to get temporally consistent grasping poses.

- An observe-to-grasp reward that encourages agents to take actions that balance observation and grasping.

- A graspability-aware mobile manipulation RL system that achieves robust performance in both simulators and real-world environments.
````

````text
Autonomous mobile manipulation has been an essential research area in robotics [1,2], leading to diverse applications, e.g., manufacturing, warehousing, construction, and household assistance [3,4,5,6]. For research on mobile manipulation, a challenging but popular task setting is to require the agent to actively observe and explore an unseen environment with the goal of manipulating a target object. Originated from the unseen nature of the environment, the agent can’t directly plan a trajectory to reach and grasp the object. Instead, its has to rely on online observations and scene priors to make a success, posing many research questions to the area.

Many existing works tackle this problem via combining 3D reconstruction and scene geometry analysis with motion planning [7,8,9]. However, such approaches usually suffer from huge computational costs for modeling the scene geometry and entail complicated heuristic designs tailored to specific robots. Recently, reinforcement learning (RL) based approaches [10,11,12,13] have gained more attention due to their simplicity and efficiency. Exemplar works [11,3] use visibility and reachability as scene priors, which can drive the agent to observe and approach the target object. They propose to learn and use them in policy’s input states as well as reward, which has significantly improved the policy performance.

In this work, we focus on an RL-based approach and propose a novel scene prior, graspability, to advance mobile manipulation policy learning. We define graspability as a complete set of valid grasping poses of the target object. Compared with reachability and visibility, which provide circuitous guidance to the agent for graspings, graspability offers more direct and informative guidance for effective grasping guidance.

Note that graspability contains the full information of the valid target object graspings, online estimating graspability in an unseen environment is highly non-trivial, e.g., online observations during mobile manipulation often include many occlusions as well as large overlaps, leading to noises and redundancy in grasping pose predictions [14]. We thus propose an online grasping pose fusion module, which dynamically fuses the redundant grasping poses and removes the outlier poses. This fusion process yields high-quality graspability estimation that achieves high precision and recall of valid grasping poses.

To facilitate agent learning, we propose the following two ways to fully utilize the estimated graspabiltity. First, we propose to encode graspabiltity into states and use it in the policy input, endowing the agent with the awareness of grasping goals. We find that our graspability-aware agent can thus learn to move its base and arm more intelligently. Second, we propose to use the number of grasps and the distance-to-grasp information in graspability as RL reward, encouraging the agent to gain more observations of valid grasping poses. We also introduce a weight schedule that combines these two rewards to balance the observation goal and the grasping goal. This reward motivates the agent to prioritize extensive observations in the initial stages, subsequently shifting its focus to grasp the target object.

Through extensive experiments on two mainstream simulators, Habitat [15] and Isaac Gym [16], which include a diverse range of environments and objects, we demonstrate that our method outperforms mainstream methods on both abstract grasping metric and realistic grasping pose metric. Moreover, real-world evaluations of our approach further showcase the robustness and effectiveness of our methodology.

In summary, the contributions of our work include:

- • We propose an online grasping fusion module to fuse predicted grasping poses to obtain temporally consistent grasping poses for erasability observation.

- • We design an observe-to-grasp reward to effectively encourage agents to execute actions that balance both observation and grasping.

- • We present a graspability-aware mobile manipulation RL system, achieving robust performance on both simulators and real-world environments.
````
