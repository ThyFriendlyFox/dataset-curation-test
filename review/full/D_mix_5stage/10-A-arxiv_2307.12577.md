<details><summary><b>Step 2 — rough_draft</b> (799 words)</summary>

````text
Deep learning has had great success in computer vision, powered by large-scale labeled natural image datasets. But annotating medical images is extremely expensive and labor-intensive. One practical approach is to pre-train a model on a large-scale labeled natural image dataset like ImageNet first, and then fine-tune it on the downstream medical image dataset that has limited annotation. However this approach may not achieve generalized performance because of the domain gap between natural images and medical images. Self-supervised learning (SSL) methods have been proposed to inherit representation from images of the same domain, by pre-training on unlabeled datasets. But it has been suggested that pre-training on unlabeled medical images only gives a relatively limited performance gain compared to ImageNet initialization. There are potentially two reasons for this: 1) The sample size of medical images, even unlabeled ones, is still quite limited compared to the ImageNet dataset; 2) Medical images often have high inter-class similarity.

Recently, vision-language pre-training (VLP) has shown that natural language supervision can effectively transfer linguistic information to visual representation through well-designed proxy tasks, such as contrastive learning and generative reconstruction. VLP may work especially well for medical image analysis, since medical reports are very likely to be accessible in most situations. Still, pre-training medical images and reports jointly is challenging. First, a medical report typically has multiple sentences, and the textual information is highly complex. Second, most descriptions in a medical report relate only to specific sub-regions in the corresponding medical image. Most existing VLP methods tend to ignore fine-grained representation and they may fail to transfer to locality-aware downstream tasks like semantic segmentation and object detection. Several methods have used local alignment losses with contrastive tasks. For example, Huang et al. design a localized feature representation framework, but it still belongs to the global alignment category. Wang et al. propose a local contrastive loss to align locality-aware information, but a vanilla contrastive loss can easily ignore the similarity among nearby sub-regions, because of their spatial locations and the overlapped sliding windows in convolution. To deal with this, Muller et al. use positiveness probability sampling so that a nearby sub-region is not selected as the negative sample. But this strategy is computationally heavy and takes a lot of time. Also, contrastive learning is a discriminative SSL paradigm that mainly focuses on high-level features, so all of these methods tend to overlook low-level features, such as lesion boundaries in images and symptom descriptions in reports, even though these are highly crucial for downstream medical image analysis tasks.

In this paper we present PRIOR, a Prototype Representation framework via joint global and local alignment between medical Images and repORts, where we combine contrastive learning and cross-modality conditional reconstruction. We treat sentences in reports and sub-regions in images as the elementary local representation units, and we get the global representation by attention pooling over the localized features. We propose a cross-modality alignment module that aligns representation between images and reports from both global and local views. To learn more locality-aware and fine-grained information, we use an encoder-decoder architecture which maximizes the conditional mutual information between paired images and reports. The reconstruction decoder tries to reconstruct the masked image given the report, and to generate the report's representation given the image. We use the prior information that the descriptions of most medical reports can essentially be summarized by multiple structured labels. This means sentence-level feature representation can be approximated by prototype categorization, and there is no need to accurately keep redundant information such as syntax. As shown in Figure 1, each sentence in a report is discretely embedded as a prototype representation. So the sentence-level representation learning process can be treated like a classification task. The image caption task predicts each word auto-regressively, but sentences in a medical report are usually non-sequential. Inspired by prior work, we use a parallel decoder to reconstruct the sentence prototype embedding through bipartite matching. We show the effectiveness of PRIOR on three challenging datasets for five downstream tasks, including supervised classification, zero-shot classification, image-to-text retrieval, semantic segmentation, and object detection.

Our main contributions are four-fold:

- • We propose a novel cross-modality alignment module that uses both global and local information to capture fine-grained features. Compared to previous works, our alignment works more effectively on local representation.

- • Considering how medical reports are written, we present a prototype memory bank for the report's sentence embeddings. This discrete representation makes the linguistic features focus more on high-level representation which is tightly linked to medical images.

- • We use a conditional reconstruction task for both vision and language representation, which further helps cross-modality feature interaction and explores more structural and causal representation.

- • PRIOR outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on five tasks, including supervised classification, zero-shot classification, image-to-text retrieval, semantic segmentation, and object detection.

Second, most descriptions in a medical report are exclusively related to specific sub-regions in the corresponding medical image [29]. For example, Huang et al. design a localized feature representation framework, but it still falls into the global alignment category [30]. Wang et al. propose a local contrastive loss to align locality-aware information [31], but a vanilla contrastive loss may easily ignore the similarity among nearby sub-regions due to their spatial locations and the overlapped sliding windows in convolution. However, this strategy is computationally heavy and time-consuming. Moreover, since contrastive learning is a discriminative SSL paradigm mainly focusing on high-level features, all those aforementioned methods tend to overlook low-level features, such as lesion boundaries in images and symptom descriptions in reports, which are nevertheless highly crucial for downstream medical image analysis tasks.

In this paper, we present a P rototype R epresentation framework via joint global and local alignment between medical I mages and rep OR ts (PRIOR), wherein we effectively combine contrastive learning and cross-modality conditional reconstruction. We consider sentence in reports and sub-region in images as the elementary local representation units, and the global representation is obtained via attention pooling over localized features. We propose a cross-modality alignment module to align representation between images and reports from both global and local views. To further learn locality-aware and fine-grained information, we utilize an encoder-decoder architecture to maximize the conditional mutual information between paired images and reports. The reconstruction decoder aims to reconstruct the masked image given the report and generate the report’s representation given the image. We make use of the prior information that descriptions of most medical reports essentially can be summarized by multiple structured labels [32]. That is, sentence-level feature representation can be approximated by prototype categorization without any need to accurately retain redundant information such as syntax. As shown in Figure 1, each sentence in a report is discretely embedded as prototype representation. In this way, the sentence-level representation learning process can be treated as a classification-like task. Different from the image caption task which predicts each word auto-regressively [33,34], sentences in a medical report are usually non-sequential. Inspired by [35], we use a parallel decoder to reconstruct sentence prototype embedding via bipartite matching. We successfully demonstrate the effectiveness of PRIOR on three challenging datasets for five downstream tasks, including supervised classification, zero-shot classification, image-to-text retrieval, semantic segmentation, and object detection.

Our main contributions are four-fold:

- • We propose a novel cross-modality alignment module utilizing both global and local information to capture fine-grained features. Compared with previous works, our proposed alignment is more effectively operated on local representation.

- • Considering the writing paradigm of a medical report, we present a prototype memory bank for report’s sentence embeddings. Such discrete representation guides the linguistic features to further focus on high-level representation that links tightly to medical images.

- • We leverage a conditional reconstruction task for both vision and language representation, which further facilitates cross-modality feature interaction and explores more structural and causal representation.

- • PRIOR outperforms existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods on five tasks, including supervised classification, zero-shot classification, image-to-text retrieval, semantic segmentation, and object detection.
