<details><summary><b>Step 2 — rough_draft</b> (590 words)</summary>

````text
AI applications of high quality are now everywhere in our society and daily lives, and at the same time the cost of getting labeled data has been skyrocketing, so unsupervised learning is getting more and more attention. Machine learning models today are more and more data-hungry and usually need a humongous amount of labeled data to reach state-of-the-art performance, and sometimes the labeling needs expert knowledge. Manual annotation needs a huge investment of resources, so unsupervised learning is naturally emerging as the best alternative.

Clustering is one of the most prominent unsupervised learning methods. The main idea of clustering is to compress the input data (like images, for computer vision problems) into lower dimensions so that the low-dimensional features can be clustered into separable groups. When the compressed features are represented better, the sample clustering process becomes more efficient. The quality of the features depends only on the dimension reduction algorithm, so how the clustering method is designed and chosen is critical for unsupervised learning to succeed. However, most real-world tasks can't easily be represented as separable low-dimensional points. Earlier attempts did classical PCA reduction before clustering, while others tried to add more features with “bags of features”; but these were mostly limited to smaller tasks. Recent works like DeepCluster explored scaling unsupervised learning by combining the k -means clustering algorithm with a standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture, and it can learn complex datasets such as ImageNet without any labels. Some works have also shown that pre-training the network, even unsupervised, helps the final model in accuracy and in convergence speed.

In this article, however, we focus on scaling unsupervised learning in a relatively nascent, bio-plausible category of neural architectures, Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs). SNNs have been gaining momentum for the next generation of edge intelligence platforms, because they have significant power, energy and latency advantages over conventional machine learning models. One of the traditional ways of training SNNs is Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP), where the model weights are updated locally based on the firing patterns of connecting neurons, which was inspired by biological measurements. STDP based learning rules have been attractive for the neuromorphic hardware community, since various emerging nanoelectronic devices have been shown to mimic STDP based learning rules through their intrinsic physics, which leads to compact and resource-efficient on-chip learning platforms. Recent works also showed that unsupervised STDP can be an energy-efficient hardware alternative to conventional clustering algorithms.

But scaling STDP trained SNNs to deeper networks and complex tasks is still a daunting task. We use insights from hybrid approaches to unsupervised deep learning like DeepCluster and aim to fill this gap so that deep unsupervised learning is possible for SNNs. Also, techniques like DeepCluster have shown promise for unsupervised learning at scale, but how the choice of the clustering method affects the learning capability and the computational requirements has not been explored. So the main contributions of the paper are:

(i) We propose a hybrid unsupervised training approach for deep convolutional networks that is compatible with SNNs and show its performance on complex recognition tasks that go beyond toy datasets like MNIST.

(ii) We show that STDP enabled deep clustering of visual features works better than the state-of-the-art k -means clustering approach, and we justify this with empirical analysis using statistical tools, namely the Fisher Information Matrix Trace, to show that STDP learns faster and more accurately.

(iii) We also give preliminary estimates comparing the computational cost of the STDP enabled Deep Clustering framework with conventional clustering methods and show the potential for significant energy savings.
````

<details><summary><b>Step 4 — final</b> (936 words)</summary>

````text
With high-quality AI applications permeating our society and daily lives, unsupervised learning is gaining increased attention as the cost of procuring labeled data has been skyrocketing concurrently. The ever-more data-hungry machine learning models usually require a humongous amount of labeled data, sometimes requiring expert knowledge, to achieve state-of-the-art performance today. Since manual annotation requires a huge investment of resources, unsupervised learning is naturally emerging as the best alternative.

One of the most prominent unsupervised learning methods is clustering. The main concept of clustering is to compress the input data (like images in the case of computer vision problems) into lower dimensions such that the low-dimensional features can be clustered into separable groups. The efficiency of the sample clustering process improves with better representations of the compressed features. Since the quality of features depends only on the dimension reduction algorithm, the design and choice of the clustering method are critical to the success of unsupervised learning. However, most real-world tasks are not easily represented as separable low-dimensional points. Earlier attempts include classical PCA reduction before clustering [1], while others attempt to augment more features with “bags of features” [2]; but mostly constrained to smaller tasks. Recent works like DeepCluster have explored scaling of unsupervised learning approaches by incorporating the k -means clustering algorithm with a standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture that can learn complex datasets such as ImageNet without any labels [3]. Some works have also proven that pre-training the network, even unsupervised, is beneficial to building the final model in terms of accuracy and convergence speed [4,5,6].

The focus of this article, however, is on scaling unsupervised learning approaches in a relatively nascent, bio-plausible category of neural architectures - Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs). SNNs have been gaining momentum for empowering the next generation of edge intelligence platforms due to their significant power, energy, and latency advantages over conventional machine learning models [7,8]. One of the traditional mechanisms of training SNNs is through Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) where the model weights are updated locally based on firing patterns of connecting neurons inspired by biological measurements [9]. STDP based learning rules have been lucrative for the neuromorphic hardware community where various emerging nanoelectronic devices have been demonstrated to mimic STDP based learning rules through their intrinsic physics, thereby leading to compact and resource-efficient on-chip learning platforms [10]. Recent works have also demonstrated that unsupervised STDP can serve as an energy-efficient hardware alternative to conventional clustering algorithms [11].

However, scaling STDP trained SNNs to deeper networks and complex tasks has remained a daunting task. Leveraging insights from hybrid approaches to unsupervised deep learning like DeepCluster [3], we aim to address this missing gap to enable deep unsupervised learning for SNNs. Further, while techniques like DeepCluster have shown promise to enable unsupervised learning at scale, the impact of the choice of the clustering method on the learning capability and computational requirements remains unexplored. The main contributions of the paper can therefore be summarized as follows:

(i) We propose a hybrid SNN-compatible unsupervised training approach for deep convolutional networks and demonstrate its performance on complex recognition tasks going beyond toy datasets like MNIST.

(ii) We demonstrate the efficacy of STDP enabled deep clustering of visual features over state-of-the-art k -means clustering approach and provide justification through empirical analysis by using statistical tools, namely Fisher Information Matrix Trace, to prove that STDP learns faster and more accurately.

(iii) We also provide preliminary computational cost estimate comparisons of the STDP enabled Deep Clustering framework against conventional clustering methods and demonstrate the potential of significant energy savings.
````
