Designing efficient reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms for large state-action space is a significant challenge in the RL community. An important part of RL is understanding the reward functions, since they directly affect the quality of the agent's policy. In some real-world situations reward distributions can be heavy-tailed, which means extremely large values happen more often than would be expected in a normal distribution. Some examples are image noise in signal processing, stock price fluctuations in financial markets, and value functions in online advertising. However, most existing RL literature assumes rewards are either uniformly bounded or light-tailed (e.g., sub-Gaussian). In these light-tailed settings the main challenge is learning the transition probabilities, so most studies assume deterministic rewards to make the analysis easier. As we will show, in heavy-tailed settings the complexity of learning the reward functions may dominate. So traditional algorithms may perform worse, and new efficient algorithms designed specifically for heavy-tailed rewards are needed.

Heavy-tailed distributions have been studied a lot in statistics and in more specific online learning scenarios such as bandits. But there is very little theoretical research in RL on heavy-tailed rewards, whose distributions only admit finite (1+\epsilon) -th moment for some \epsilon\in(0,1]. One notable exception is a previous work that made a pioneering effort to establish worst-case regret guarantees in tabular Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with heavy-tailed rewards. But their algorithm can't handle RL settings with large state-action space. Also, they rely on truncation-based methods, which is sub-optimal because these methods depend heavily on raw moments, and raw moments do not vanish in deterministic cases. So a natural question is:

Can we derive sample and time-efficient algorithms for RL with large state-action space that achieve instance-dependent regret in the presence of heavy-tailed rewards?

In this work we look at linear MDPs with heavy-tailed rewards and we answer the question above affirmatively. We say a distribution is heavy-tailed if it only admits finite (1+\epsilon) -th moment for some \epsilon\in(0,1]. Our contributions are as follows.

- We first propose Heavy-OFUL, a computationally efficient algorithm for heavy-tailed linear bandits. This setting can be seen as a special case of linear MDPs. Heavy-OFUL gets an instance-dependent T -round regret of \widetilde{O}\big(dT^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2(1+\epsilon)}}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\nu_{t}^{2}}+dT^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2(1+\epsilon)}}\big), which is the first of this kind. Here d is the feature dimension and \nu_{t}^{1+\epsilon} is the (1+\epsilon) -th central moment of the reward at the t -th round. The main term of the instance-dependent regret bound only depends on the sum of central moments, so it does not have a \sqrt{T} term. We show our regret bound is minimax optimal in both stochastic and deterministic linear bandits (See Remark 4.2 for details).

- Then we extend this algorithm to time-inhomogeneous linear MDPs with heavy-tailed rewards, and get a new computationally efficient algorithm Heavy-LSVI-UCB. For the first time it achieves a K -episode regret scaling as \widetilde{O}(d\sqrt{H\mathcal{U}^{*}}K^{\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}}+d\sqrt{H\mathcal{V}^{*}K}). Here H is the length of the episode and \mathcal{U}^{*},\mathcal{V}^{*} are quantities that measure the central moment of the reward functions and of the transition probabilities, respectively (See Theorem 5.2 for details). The regret bound is instance-dependent because the main term only depends on instance-dependent quantities, and it vanishes when the dynamics and rewards are deterministic. In special cases our instance-dependent regret recovers the variance-aware regret in previous work (See Remark 5.3 for details) and it improves existing first-order regret bounds (See Corollary 5.6 for details).

- We give a minimax regret lower bound \Omega(dHK^{\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}}+d\sqrt{H^{3}K}) for linear MDPs with heavy-tailed rewards. It matches the worst-case regret bound implied by our instance-dependent regret, which shows that Heavy-LSVI-UCB is minimax optimal in the worst case.

We summarize the regrets for linear bandits and linear MDPs in Table 1 and 2 respectively, to compare our algorithms with state-of-the-art results. More related works are in Appendix A. Our results show that \epsilon=1 (i.e. finite variance) is enough to get variance-aware regret bounds of the same order as when rewards are uniformly bounded, for both linear bandits and linear MDPs. The main technical contribution behind our results is a new robust self-normalized concentration inequality, inspired by adaptive Huber regression. More specifically, it is a non-trivial generalization of adaptive Huber regression from the independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) case to heavy-tailed online regression settings, and it gives a self-normalized bound instead of the \ell_{2} -norm bound in the original work. Our result is computationally efficient and only scales with the feature dimension, d, the (1+\epsilon) -th central moment of the noise, \nu, and it does not depend on the absolute magnitude like other self-normalized concentration inequalities do.
